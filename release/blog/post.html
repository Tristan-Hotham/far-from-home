Fly to Frankfurt and land 103 km away. Take a train to Llanbister Road and walk the rest. I measured how far every airport in the world and every railway station in the UK stands from the place in its name, and checked every long case by hand, because that is where the data lies to you.

Anyone who has flown on a cheap ticket knows the feeling. The booking says Frankfurt, and you land at Hahn, in the hills of the Hunsrück. The booking says Paris, and you land at Beauvais, 69 km out. The booking says Barcelona, and Reus is 88 km down the coast. London Oxford Airport is 90 km from London. I wanted to know how far a name can stretch, and whether railway stations do it too.

So I took 4,448 airports from Wikidata that have a passenger code and are named after a place, and 2,888 railway stations and halts in the UK from OpenStreetMap whose name starts with a town or village, and measured the straight line from each one to the place in its name.

Most names are honest

Half of all airports are within 5.6 km of the place they are named after, and only 3% are more than 30 km away. Stations are tighter still: half stand within 0.6 km of the town in their name. The far ones are a small club, and nearly all of them are far for the same few reasons.

The farthest from the city on the ticket

The farthest airport I could confirm is Châlons Vatry in Champagne, sold for years as Paris-Vatry, 136 km from Paris. Then come Chicago Rockford (126 km from Chicago), Tongren Dejiang in China (110 km), Frankfurt-Hahn (103 km) and Memmingen, which airlines sell as Munich West (101 km). Warsaw Radom, London Oxford, Stockholm Skavsta, New York Stewart and Barcelona Reus are all between 85 and 95 km out.

{{IMG|b_world.png|Bar chart of the fifteen airports farthest from their city: Paris Vatry 136 km, Chicago Rockford 126 km, Tongren Dejiang 110 km, Frankfurt-Hahn 103 km, Memmingen 101 km and on down to Rize-Artvin in Turkey at 81 km, most in red because a closer airport serves the same city|The farthest airports from the city they name. Red means another airport named after the same city is closer to it.}}

Twelve of those fifteen are second airports: another airport named after the same city is closer to it. Stockholm Skavsta is 11.4 times as far from Stockholm as Bromma, and Hahn is 8.8 times as far from Frankfurt as Frankfurt's own airport. Many began as military airfields, and the low-cost airlines that moved in sold them under the name of the big city nearby. China has its own habit: airports there often carry the name of the prefecture as well as the county they stand in, which is how Tongren Dejiang ends up 110 km from Tongren. And some airports serve two places at once: Rize-Artvin, on the Black Sea coast of Turkey, is 81 km from Artvin.

{{IMG|b_europe.png|Map of Europe with coloured lines from airports to the cities they are named after, the longest from Vatry to Paris, Hahn to Frankfurt, Memmingen to Munich, Radom to Warsaw, Oxford to London, Skavsta to Stockholm and Reus to Barcelona, with a numbered list beside it|Europe's stretched airport names: every named airport 30 km or more from its city.}}

London is a long way from London Oxford

In these islands London stretches furthest. Besides Oxford at 90 km there is London Southend at 57 km, Stansted at 49 km, Luton at 44 km and Gatwick at 40 km. Heathrow, at 23 km, is the close one. Outside London the longest stretch is Glasgow Prestwick, 45 km from Glasgow. Airports in the Republic of Ireland, by contrast, all sit within 8 km of their towns; the ones with wider names, like Donegal and Connemara, are named after a county or a region.

Road, Parkway and Airport

Stations stretch less, but they have a vocabulary for it. The farthest from their town's centre are the airport stations: Manchester Airport (12.8 km), Birmingham International (12.4 km) and Inverness Airport (11.6 km). But those are named after the airport, not the town, so I set them aside. Big cities sprawl, too, so I also measured how far each station stands beyond a rough circle round its town. On that count Tiverton Parkway comes first, 9.1 km from Tiverton, then Llanbister Road in mid Wales (6.7 km), Dent, high above Dentdale (5.8 km), and Beaulieu Road in the New Forest (5.5 km).

{{IMG|b_stations_map.png|Map of Great Britain with thousands of pale dots for stations close to the town in their name, and ten numbered red stations far from theirs, including Tiverton Parkway, Llanbister Road, Dent, Beaulieu Road and Irton Road|UK railway stations by how far they stand from the town or village in their name.}}

The Victorians had an honest word for a station some way off: Road. Many of the 82 stations with Road in their name are named after a street, like Tottenham Court Road. But 9 are named after a village 2 km or more down the road: Llanbister Road, Beaulieu Road, Irton Road, Roughton Road, Wittersham Road, Builth Road, Clarbeston Road, Farningham Road and Harling Road. At Builth Road and Clarbeston Road a new village grew up round the station and took its name, so the station is now named after itself. The modern word is Parkway: half of the 25 parkways are within 2.2 km of their town, but Tiverton Parkway is 9.1 km out and Bristol Parkway 7.7 km.

How I did this, and where the errors hide

For airports I used the places each airport serves in Wikidata, but kept a place only if the airport is actually named after it: its name has to be in the airport's label. Wikidata now labels some airports by their local name (Hahn Airport, not Frankfurt-Hahn), so I added 11 names that airlines sold them under, listed in the paper. For stations I took the longest run of words at the start of the name that matches a town or village in Wikidata, and measured to the nearest place of that name.

Then I read every long case by eye, and I am glad I did. Of 113 airport cases over 40 km, 23 were simply wrong: Bathurst Island Airfield in northern Australia linked to Bathurst in New South Wales, 3,000 km away; Wilmington in North Carolina linked to Wilmington in Delaware; two Yichuns, two Nagoyas, two Heringsdorfs. Another 12 were named after a region or an island, and 12 I could not settle and left out. Of 286 station matches over 3 km, 208 were a different place with the same name, usually because the real namesake is missing from the list under that name, and 7 were streets. In all, 42% of the long airport cases and 79% of the long station cases were not what the data first said.

{{IMG|b_checks.png|Two stacked bars: of 113 airport cases over 40 km, 66 were kept and the rest were wrong links, regions or unsure; of 286 station matches over 3 km, 61 were kept and 215 were another place of the same name or a street|What reading every long case by eye found.}}

The usual caveats apply. A straight line is not a journey, and a city's point in Wikidata is its centre, not its edge. Populations are for the city itself (Paris is the two million inside the périphérique, not the twelve million around it). The list of airline names is my own, checked against airline and airport listings, and several of these airports have since dropped the big city's name. Wikidata also keeps closed airports, such as Oslo's old Fornebu, so a city's closer airport is not always one you can still fly to. If you know a station named after somewhere even further away, I would love to hear about it.
